## What and Why?

We want to build **Jargonot**, a mobile web application that makes news articles and research papers easier to understand for high school and undergraduate students.

Many students are curious about topics such as artificial intelligence, finance, economics, international policy, science, technology, health, and other rapidly changing fields. However, the information available in these areas is often written for experts. News articles may assume that readers already understand important terminology, while research papers can be filled with technical language, unfamiliar concepts, and complicated structures.

Because of this, students who are genuinely interested in learning about a topic may become discouraged before they can fully understand it. They may know that an important development has happened without understanding what actually happened, why it matters, or what background knowledge they are missing.

Jargonot aims to reduce that barrier.

Instead of simply shortening an article or research paper, Jargonot will help readers understand it by:

* translating difficult jargon into accessible language;
* explaining important terms and keywords;
* providing necessary background information;
* showing why a development matters;
* allowing readers to explore a topic at different levels of depth;
* providing a link back to the original source for users who want to continue reading.

The goal is not to replace serious news or research papers. Instead, Jargonot will act as a bridge between a curious student and information that may currently feel too technical or intimidating.

Over time, we want students to gradually build enough vocabulary and background knowledge that they become more comfortable engaging with advanced sources directly.

## For Whom?

Jargonot is primarily designed for **high school and undergraduate students who are curious about important developments in the world but do not yet have expert-level knowledge in the subjects they are reading about**.

Our initial users would be students around us, including friends and classmates who are interested in subjects outside of their current area of study.

For example:

* a computer science student who wants to understand finance;
* a high school student interested in artificial intelligence;
* an engineering student trying to follow international policy;
* a student interested in scientific research but unfamiliar with academic writing;
* someone who wants to understand an important news story without repeatedly searching for definitions of unfamiliar terms.

We are also part of the target audience ourselves. As students, we often encounter articles and research papers that seem interesting but require knowledge of several concepts before the main idea becomes understandable.

This gives us access to real potential users whom we can interview and test the application with throughout the semester.

Although the platform could eventually support a very large number of subjects, we would expand the available fields gradually rather than attempting to fully cover every area immediately.

## How?

When a user opens Jargonot, they will be able to discover news stories and research papers based on the subjects they are interested in.

The platform may include topics such as technology, artificial intelligence, business, finance, economics, science, politics, international affairs, health, and many other fields as the platform grows.

Instead of showing only the original article or paper, Jargonot will break the information into a more approachable format.

A typical news item might include:

### What happened?

A short explanation of the main event or development using understandable language.

### Why does it matter?

A brief explanation of why the story is important and how it may affect the field or the wider world.

### What should I know?

Important terms and background concepts that the reader may need in order to understand the story.

For example, if a finance article mentions the Federal Reserve, inflation, bond yields, and monetary policy, users could select those terms and receive short explanations without having to leave the article and search for them separately.

### Go deeper

Users who are interested in learning more could open a more detailed explanation of the story or concept.

### Original source

Users would always be able to access the original news article, research paper, or other source.

This creates different levels of reading:

**Quick overview** → understand the main idea.

**Context** → understand the terminology and background.

**Go deeper** → learn more about the topic.

**Original source** → engage directly with the original material.

Research papers would have a similar but slightly different structure. Instead of presenting a dense abstract immediately, Jargonot could answer questions such as:

* What question were the researchers trying to answer?
* What did they do?
* What did they discover?
* Why is the result important?
* What concepts should I understand before reading the paper?
* What limitations does the research have?

Users could also save articles and papers that interest them and keep track of important terms they have learned.

As the application develops, Jargonot could become more personalized. For example, if a user already understands a particular concept, the application would not need to explain it repeatedly. Instead, it could focus on unfamiliar concepts and gradually provide more advanced explanations as the user's knowledge grows.

The long-term goal is for Jargonot to not only understand **what a user is interested in**, but also **what background knowledge they may need in order to understand it**.

## Scope

Jargonot is large enough to be a meaningful project for a team of approximately 4–6 programmers while still being achievable within one semester.

A semester version of the application could focus on the core user experience, including:

* user accounts;
* selection of topics and interests;
* a feed of news articles and research papers;
* simplified explanations of difficult content;
* explanations of important terms and jargon;
* background information and context;
* a "why this matters" section;
* different levels of detail;
* saving articles and papers;
* links to original sources;
* basic tracking of topics or concepts a user has encountered.

The application does not need to completely cover every field or automatically understand every possible research paper during the first semester. The platform can be designed so that additional topics and subject areas can be added gradually.

More advanced features, such as highly personalized knowledge profiles, automatically adjusting explanation difficulty, detailed knowledge maps, social features, quizzes, or recommendation algorithms, could be added later if time allows.

This makes the project challenging enough to require meaningful work across user experience, content organization, personalization, data storage, and application design without making the initial version unrealistically large.

## Vision

We believe that curiosity should not be limited by unfamiliar vocabulary or a lack of expert background knowledge.

Jargonot aims to help students move from:

> "This looks interesting, but I don't understand it."

to:

> "I understand what happened, why it matters, and I know enough to learn more."

Our goal is to make complex information more approachable without removing the ideas that make it meaningful.
